Understand the Risk Before You Sign

Commercial Risk Management for Nuclear Contracts

Parent company guarantees, performance bonds and letters of credit aren't just paperwork — they're how nuclear buyers transfer risk onto suppliers. Know what you're agreeing to before you agree to it.

7 min readTraining & Development

01Why commercial risk needs its own system

Nuclear contracts carry commercial risk that general commercial experience doesn't always prepare you for — long programme durations, high-consequence failure modes, and buyers who routinely require formal security instruments as a condition of contract. Treating commercial risk management as a system, not a one-off legal review, is what separates SMEs that get burned by a bad clause from those that don't.

02Building sector contractual knowledge

  • Understand the standard contract forms used across the sector (e.g. NEC, bespoke nuclear buyer terms) before you're negotiating one under time pressure.
  • Know which clauses are genuinely standard versus which are buyer-specific additions worth pushing back on.
  • Build institutional knowledge that survives one person leaving — don't let contract expertise live in a single individual's head.

03Common security provisions in nuclear contracts

Parent company guarantee (PCG)

A guarantee from a supplier's parent company to cover its obligations if the contracting entity fails to deliver — relevant if you operate as part of a group structure.

Performance bond

A financial instrument, usually provided by a bank or insurer, that pays out to the buyer if the supplier fails to perform — understand the cost and working-capital impact of holding one before agreeing to it.

Letter of credit

A bank's guarantee of payment, often used to provide the buyer assurance of a supplier's financial standing or to secure advance payments.

04Building a contract commercial risk system

Establish a standard contract review processDefine who reviews what, and at what value threshold escalation to specialist legal advice is required — don't leave it to individual judgement on a case-by-case basis.
Track security provisions across your live contract bookKnow your total live exposure across all bonds, guarantees and letters of credit at any point in time, not just per-contract.
Understand the financial and working-capital impact before signingA performance bond ties up facility headroom for the life of the contract — model this impact before committing, not after.
Build organisational risk management systems that outlive individual contractsA risk register, an escalation process and a lessons-learned loop that apply across your whole contract book, not reinvented for each new deal.

05Organisational risk management systems

The strongest suppliers don't manage commercial risk contract-by-contract — they run a standing organisational system: a live risk register, clear escalation thresholds, and a habit of feeding contract-close lessons back into how the next contract is reviewed.

06Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Signing a performance bond or parent company guarantee without modelling its impact on working capital and banking facilities.
✗Treating each new contract's commercial terms as a one-off review, with no institutional memory of past negotiating positions.
✗Not distinguishing between genuinely standard sector terms and buyer-specific clauses that are actually negotiable.
✗Escalating every contract to expensive external legal review rather than building in-house capability for standard terms.

07Frequently asked questions

Can I negotiate a performance bond down or out entirely?

Sometimes — smaller-value or shorter-duration contracts, or a strong track record with a buyer, can support negotiating the requirement down; it's rarely worth assuming it's fixed without asking.

What's the practical difference between a PCG and a performance bond?

A PCG relies on your parent company's own covenant strength and doesn't tie up cash directly; a performance bond usually requires facility headroom with a bank or insurer and has a direct cost.
