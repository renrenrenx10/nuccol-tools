A non-technical checklist

Cyber Security Essentials for Manufacturers

You don't need to be a cyber expert to get the basics right. Five control areas, explained in plain English, with the reasoning behind each one.

7 min readSecurity

01Firewalls

Boundary controls that stop unauthorised traffic reaching your network — think of it as a locked front door for your internet connection.

  • Firewall enabled on all internet connections
  • Default admin passwords changed from the factory setting

02Secure Configuration

Devices and software set up securely from the start, rather than left on their factory defaults, which are often designed for ease of setup rather than security.

  • Unnecessary software and accounts removed from devices
  • Auto-run features disabled so untrusted media can't execute automatically

03Security Update Management

Keeping software patched against known vulnerabilities — most breaches exploit gaps that a routine update would already have closed.

  • Operating systems and software kept up to date
  • Unsupported software identified and replaced before it becomes a liability

04User Access Control

Making sure people only have access to what they actually need to do their job — the fewer people with wide access, the smaller your exposure if an account is compromised.

  • Unique logins for every user — no shared accounts
  • Admin rights limited to those who genuinely need them

05Malware Protection

Defending against viruses and other malicious software that can spread through email, removable media or compromised websites.

  • Anti-malware software installed and kept current
  • Staff know not to open unexpected attachments or links, even from familiar-looking senders

06Why this matters beyond compliance

Security of Information is one of the seven F4N categories precisely because a weak link here doesn't just risk your own data — it can compromise the safety and security of the wider nuclear programme you're supplying into. A single compromised laptop holding design drawings can have consequences well beyond your own business.

A realistic scenarioAn employee's laptop is infected via a phishing email and technical drawings for a nuclear component are exposed. Even if no safety impact results, this is a reportable security event with real consequences for your customer relationship and your F4N status.

07Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be Cyber Essentials certified to work in nuclear?

Not universally required for every contract, but it's widely expected across the supply chain and maps directly onto the five themes above.

We're a very small business — is this overkill for us?

The five controls above are deliberately basic and proportionate — they're the minimum expected of any business handling sensitive information, regardless of size.

Cyber Essentials is the recognised next step

UK Cyber Essentials certification maps closely onto these five control themes and is widely expected across the nuclear supply chain.
