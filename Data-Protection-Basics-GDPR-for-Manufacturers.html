GDPR for manufacturers

Data Protection Basics

The practical basics of UK GDPR compliance, without the legal jargon — what it actually requires of a typical manufacturing SME.

7 min readLegal & Compliance
This guide is general information, not legal advice. Always seek specific guidance for your own situation.

01The seven principles

PrincipleIn plain English
Lawfulness, fairness, transparencyHave a valid reason to hold data, and be open with people about it
Purpose limitationOnly use data for the reason you originally collected it
Data minimisationOnly collect what you actually need, not everything that might be useful someday
AccuracyKeep data correct and up to date, and correct it when you learn it's wrong
Storage limitationDon't keep data longer than necessary for the purpose you collected it for
Integrity and confidentialityKeep data secure against loss, misuse or unauthorised access
AccountabilityBe able to show you're complying, not just claim it — this usually means having some record of your decisions

02Controller vs processor — why the distinction matters

A controller decides why and how personal data is processed. A processor handles data on a controller's behalf, such as a payroll provider or IT support company. Most manufacturers are controllers for their own staff and customer data, and may additionally be processors if they handle data for a client's employees, for example. Knowing which role you're in for a given piece of data determines what your obligations actually are.

03A practical checklist

  • Know what personal data you hold, and why you're holding it
  • Have a basic privacy notice available to staff and customers, explaining what you do with their data
  • Have a process for responding to a data access request within the required timeframe
  • Report any serious data breach within 72 hours where required by law

04Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Collecting more personal data than is actually needed, 'just in case'.
✗Having no clear process for what to do if someone asks what data you hold on them.
✗Assuming GDPR only applies to large organisations with big customer databases.

05Frequently asked questions

Does GDPR really apply to a small manufacturer like us?

Yes — if you hold personal data on staff, customers or suppliers, the principles above apply regardless of company size.

What counts as a 'serious' data breach?

Broadly, one likely to result in risk to people's rights and freedoms — if in doubt, treat it seriously and seek advice rather than assuming it's too minor to matter.

Keep it proportionate

Data protection doesn't need to be complicated for a typical SME — the basics above cover most day-to-day risk.
