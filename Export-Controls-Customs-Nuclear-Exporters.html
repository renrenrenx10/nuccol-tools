Export Controls & Customs for Nuclear Exporters

The key concepts you need to know before exporting nuclear-relevant goods or technology, explained in plain English.

7 min readLegal & Compliance
This guide is general information, not legal advice. Always seek specific guidance for your own situation before acting.

01Why this applies even if you feel like a small player

Export controls apply based on what you're exporting and to whom, not on the size of your business. Many SME manufacturers are surprised to learn that a component that seems generic can be classed as dual-use or nuclear-related once its intended application is considered — which is exactly why checking early matters more than assuming you're too small to be affected.

02Key concepts

Dual-use items

Goods or technology with both civilian and military/strategic applications — the same part can be entirely benign in one context and controlled in another, depending on its end use.

Strategic export controls

UK rules restricting the export of sensitive goods, software and technology, administered separately from ordinary customs processes.

Commodity codes

Codes used to classify goods for customs and control purposes — getting the wrong code can trigger delays or compliance issues even for a routine shipment.

Sanctions regimes

Restrictions on trade with specific countries, entities or individuals, which can change with little notice and need to be checked at the time of each shipment.

SPIRE

The UK's system for applying for export licences, used when a licence is required before goods can be shipped.

AEO (Authorised Economic Operator)

A trusted-trader customs status that can simplify and speed up customs processes for businesses that hold it.

03A sensible starting checklist

  • Check whether what you're exporting could be classed as dual-use or nuclear-related, even if it seems generic at first glance.
  • Screen your customer and destination country against current sanctions lists before committing to an order.
  • Confirm the correct commodity code before shipping, rather than reusing a code from a previous, similar shipment without checking.
  • Establish whether an export licence is required before you commit to a contract, not after goods are ready to ship.

04Frequently asked questions

How do I know if my product needs an export licence?

This depends on the specific goods, their classification, and the destination — specialist export control guidance is the right route to a definitive answer, not a general assumption either way.

What happens if I get this wrong?

Export control breaches can carry serious legal and financial consequences, which is exactly why checking in advance, even for routine-seeming exports, is worth the time.
