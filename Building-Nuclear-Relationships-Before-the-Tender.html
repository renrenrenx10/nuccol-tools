Why the ITT Isn't Where It Starts

Building Nuclear Relationships Before the Tender

By the time a tender is published, the buyer's preferred delivery model, likely partners and credible suppliers are often already understood. Here's how that early picture forms &mdash; and how to be part of it.

9 min readWinning Work

01The uncomfortable truth about tenders

Formal competitive tendering has to be fair and transparent once it starts — that's the law, and rightly so. But "once it starts" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. Buyers routinely engage the market for months, sometimes a year or more, before a tender notice is ever published. If your first contact with a buyer is the Invitation to Tender itself, you're starting the relationship at the exact moment your competitors who engaged earlier are finishing theirs.

This is deliberately built into procurement law, not a grey area to be wary of. The Procurement Act 2023 explicitly enables and encourages preliminary market engagement — provided it doesn't give any one supplier an unfair advantage or distort competition.

02The mechanisms buyers actually use

MechanismWhat it isWhen it happens
Prior Information Notice (PIN)An early notice signalling a buyer's intention to procure, without committing anyone to bid. Responding registers your interest and can influence how the procurement is designed.35–365 days before the formal contract notice — sometimes much earlier
Pre-Market Engagement (PME)Formal consultation with suppliers to test specifications and understand market capability, disclosed in the eventual contract notice for transparency.Typically 3–12 months before the formal notice
Supplier Engagement DayA structured, buyer-hosted event — presentations in the morning, workshops or breakouts in the afternoon — covering technical, commercial and social value themes.Ahead of major programmes, framework refreshes or complex procurements
Expression of Interest (EOI)An initial, non-binding signal of interest, often submitted in response to a PIN or engagement event. It's not a bid and isn't scored — it puts you on the distribution list for what follows.As the first step of a two-stage process, or after a PIN/engagement event
Pipeline NoticeA forward look at a buyer's planned procurements over a longer horizon, useful for planning which relationships to prioritise well in advancePublished periodically, often annually

03What this looks like from a real nuclear buyer

Sellafield Ltd's own Supply Chain Strategy update makes the mechanism explicit: rather than simply publishing a tender and waiting for bids, it runs structured supply chain engagement well ahead of major contract awards, and is explicit that its major delivery models are designed for the wider supply chain to benefit, not just the handful of prime contractors named at the top.

A live public exampleNuclear Waste Services has published a genuine Market Engagement PIN for its Inventory & Wasteform Evolution Framework on Find a Tender — inviting the market to respond and shape the eventual procurement before the formal tender exists. This is exactly the kind of notice worth actively watching for in your own specialism, rather than only searching Find a Tender once a live opportunity appears.

At Hinkley Point C, EDF's Supply Chain Engagement team (run jointly with the Somerset Chamber of Commerce) proactively identifies and engages local and national businesses and makes introductions to the Tier 1 contractors already delivering the project — a relationship-first model rather than a tender-first one.

04What good early engagement actually looks like

  • Responding to PINs and pipeline notices in your specialism, even when a formal tender is a year away — this is how you get onto the radar before anyone is deciding who to shortlist.
  • Attending Supplier Engagement Days and asking substantive questions about scope and technical requirements, not just collecting a brochure.
  • Submitting a genuine, specific Expression of Interest rather than a generic capability statement — buyers use EOI volume to judge whether their procurement design will attract real competition.
  • Building the relationship with the right level — procurement teams manage the formal process, but technical and programme staff often shape the requirement long before procurement is even involved.
  • Being visible between opportunities — through NucCol events, F4N certification and the Nuclear Supplier Database — so you're a known quantity, not a cold approach, when engagement does begin.

05The line you can't cross

Early engagement must never give one supplier an unfair advantage over another — this is a legal requirement under the Procurement Act 2023, not just good etiquette. Buyers must disclose their pre-market engagement activity in the eventual contract notice, and any information shared with one supplier that materially affects the competition generally has to be shared with all bidders. Engage openly, ask genuine questions, and expect the buyer to manage the process carefully — that's a sign it's being done properly, not a barrier to worry about.

06A realistic first step

Step 1 — Identify your target buyers and watch their pipelinesNDA, Sellafield, EDF and Nuclear Waste Services all publish forward-looking procurement information — start watching the ones relevant to your capability, not just Find a Tender's live opportunities.
Step 2 — Respond to PINs and market engagement invitations genuinelyTreat an EOI or PIN response as seriously as a bid — it's the first thing a buyer sees from you, and first impressions here carry forward.
Step 3 — Show up to Supplier Engagement Days preparedBring specific, informed questions about the scope, not generic sales material — this is a technical and capability conversation, not a marketing pitch.
Step 4 — Keep the relationship warm between opportunitiesMeet the Buyer events, sector Summits and NucCol's own network all exist to keep you visible in the gaps between formal procurement activity.

07Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Only searching Find a Tender for live opportunities, and missing PINs and pipeline notices that signal what's coming months in advance.
✗Treating an Expression of Interest as a formality rather than the first real impression a buyer forms of you.
✗Assuming pre-market engagement is only for large Tier 1 contractors, when it's specifically designed to also surface capable SMEs the buyer doesn't already know about.
✗Going quiet between opportunities, so the buyer has to rediscover you from scratch each time a new procurement starts.

08Frequently asked questions

Is it legal for a buyer to talk to me before a tender exists?

Yes — the Procurement Act 2023 explicitly provides for preliminary market engagement, provided it's conducted fairly and doesn't distort the eventual competition. It's a recognised, encouraged part of the process, not a workaround.

What's the difference between a PIN and an Expression of Interest?

A PIN is published by the buyer to signal an upcoming procurement. An Expression of Interest is submitted by a supplier, usually in response to a PIN or an engagement event, to register interest — the PIN comes from the buyer, the EOI comes from you.

Will engaging early guarantee I win the eventual tender?

No — the formal tender still has to be fair and competitive, and everyone eligible gets to bid. Early engagement improves your chances by making you known, credible and better informed about the real requirement, not by guaranteeing the outcome.

Where do I find PINs and pipeline notices for nuclear work?

Find a Tender publishes PINs and Market Engagement notices publicly. NDA, Sellafield and other site licensees also publish their own procurement pipelines and plan schedules directly on their websites.
