How to use this

Six numbers a month.
That is the whole job.

You type the raw counts you already have on a clipboard somewhere. We do the arithmetic, keep the history, and show you how you compare with other suppliers on the programme. Nobody sees your figures unless you decide they should.

1
Choose what you track Once · about 5 minutes

Go to Your metrics. Five are already ticked — the ones most companies start with, and the only ones where you are likely to get a comparison straight away. Add anything else you already measure, and leave the rest.

Set a target beside any metric where you have one. A target is yours alone: it is never shared, never compared, and never seen by NucCol.

Do not over-pick Every metric you tick is more typing every month. The panel on the right tells you how many numbers your choices will ask for.
2
Add your numbers Monthly · about 2 minutes

Go to Add this month. We only ever ask for raw counts — deliveries due, deliveries on time, units made, units scrapped. Never a percentage. The panel on the right fills in as you type, so you can see the result before you save.

Last month's figure sits beside each box. If this month looks very different, that is usually a typo rather than a crisis.

Why raw counts? So every company's percentage is worked out the same way. It is also less typing for you.
3
Read your dashboard Whenever you like

Each figure is shown three ways at once: against your own target, against your own history, and against the middle of the group. A green dot means you are meeting your target; red means you are not. The dashed line on a chart is your target.

Use One at a time to dig into a single number — you get the chart, where you sit in the group, and the raw figures you entered. Use All at once to sweep the lot.

No comparison showing? We only show one when at least five other companies have submitted that same figure. Fewer than that and you could work out who is who.
4
Build a view and send it somewhere As needed

A view is a saved set of metrics in the order you want them, drawn how you want them. Build one for your Monday standup, one for the board, one for the workshop screen.

The same view then produces the dashboard, the printed pack, a read-only link for a screen on the shop floor, and a monthly email — so there is one thing to keep up to date rather than four.

Screens The screen link needs no login and shows only that view, so you can leave it running on a TV without worrying what else is on it.

What we do with your numbers

What NucCol can see

  • The group median and spread, worked out across everyone — never traced back to a company.
  • Your individual figures, but only if you switch on sharing with your consultant, and only them.
  • How many companies are submitting each metric, so we know when a comparison can be shown.

What it never sees

  • Your targets. They are a statement of intent, not performance, and they stay with you.
  • Your figures in any report, named or identifiable — aggregates only, never below five companies.
  • Anything at all, if you opt out of the comparison. You keep your own dashboard either way.

Questions people ask

I missed a month. Have I ruined it?

No. The month sits there waiting and you can fill it in late. While it is missing, your dashboard shows the last month you did submit and says so, and you are left out of that month's group comparison rather than counted as a zero.

We do not measure one of these. What do I put?

Nothing — untick it. You only track what you actually measure. The five core metrics are a starting point, not a requirement, though they are where the comparison is most useful.

Can I change my metrics later?

Yes, any time, under Your metrics. Anything you add starts its history from that month, so a metric you add in March will not have a trend until the summer.

I got a number wrong and already saved it.

Correct it and save again. The dashboard shows that the figure was amended and when, and the group comparison is recalculated. Corrections are normal and nobody is marked down for them.

Who in the business should be doing this?

Whoever already has the numbers — usually an operations or quality manager. It is deliberately small enough that it does not need to be anyone's job.
