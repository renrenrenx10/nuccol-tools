A tip sheet for growing manufacturers

Skills Pipeline Planning

Five practical steps to make sure your skills pipeline keeps pace with nuclear-scale growth, with the detail behind each one.

7 min readSkills & Workforce

01Build a skills matrix — before you need it

A simple grid of who can do what, and to what level, turns skills gaps from a surprise into a visible, manageable risk. List every critical task down one side and every team member across the top, then mark each cell as trained, competent, or expert. Start this week with a spreadsheet, not a system — the value is in having it exist, not in how sophisticated it looks.

What it revealsMost SMEs discover through this exercise that one or two people hold knowledge nobody else has. That's the single-point-of-failure risk the next tip addresses directly.

02Plan for vetting lead time

Nuclear-sector vetting and security clearances can take weeks or, in some cases, months longer than a typical recruitment process. Build that lead time into your recruitment planning now, rather than discovering it once a contract is already signed and the clock is running against you.

  • Ask your customer early what level of clearance or vetting their contract will require.
  • Start the process as soon as an offer is accepted, not once someone starts work.
  • Keep a small buffer of pre-vetted contingency staff if your workload is likely to spike.

03Cross-train your critical roles

Use your skills matrix to identify the two or three roles where a single point of failure would hurt most — typically a specialist machine operator, a quality sign-off role, or someone holding an unwritten process in their head. Start cross-training a second person into each of them, even at a basic level.

04Keep competence records, not just certificates

Buyers increasingly want evidence of ongoing competence, not just a certificate on a wall from several years ago. A simple log of training completed, sign-offs given, and refreshers scheduled goes a long way in an audit, and is far easier to produce on the day if it's kept up to date continuously rather than reconstructed after the fact.

05Use the sector's skills support, rather than solving this alone

The National Nuclear Strategic Plan for Skills exists precisely to help companies like yours plan and fund training, apprenticeships and career pathways into the sector. Many SMEs aren't aware this support exists, or assume it's only for larger organisations — it isn't.

06A simple 90-day starting plan

Weeks 1–2Build your skills matrix and identify your top single-point-of-failure risks.
Weeks 3–6Start cross-training on your highest-risk role; begin a simple competence-record log.
Weeks 7–12Contact a sector skills body about funding or apprenticeship support relevant to your gaps.

07Frequently asked questions

We're a small team — is a skills matrix overkill for us?

The smaller the team, the more a single-point-of-failure risk can hurt you. A basic matrix for even five or six people is worth the hour it takes to build.

Where do I find out about nuclear-sector skills funding?
