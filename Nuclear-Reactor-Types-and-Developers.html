Know Your Market Before You Pitch

Nuclear Reactor Types and Their Developers

PWR, SMR, CANDU, Gen IV — the acronyms matter because each reactor type has a different developer, different supply chain and different point of entry for an SME.

7 min readTraining & Development

01Why reactor type matters to your pitch

Nuclear isn't one market — it's several, each built around a different reactor technology with its own design authority, supply chain structure and procurement rhythm. Knowing which reactor type your target customer is building changes what you should be pitching and to whom.

02The main reactor types

TypeHow it worksWhere it's used
PWR (Pressurised Water Reactor)Water kept under high pressure to stop it boiling, transferring heat via a steam generator to a secondary loop that drives the turbineThe most common reactor type globally, including Hinkley Point C and Sizewell C (EPR design)
BWR (Boiling Water Reactor)Water boils directly in the reactor core, producing steam that drives the turbine directly — simpler design, one less heat-exchange loopUsed internationally, notably by GE Hitachi designs
CANDU (Canada Deuterium Uranium)Uses heavy water as both coolant and moderator, allowing it to run on natural (unenriched) uraniumCanadian-developed, used internationally including parts of the UK's historic fleet lineage
Gas-Cooled ReactorUses gas (historically carbon dioxide) rather than water as coolantThe UK's AGR (Advanced Gas-Cooled Reactor) fleet, now largely retiring/decommissioning
Fast Neutron ReactorUses fast, unmoderated neutrons, enabling different fuel cycles including reuse of spent fuelEmerging and advanced designs, relevant to future Gen IV programmes
SMR (Small Modular Reactor)Smaller-output reactors built largely from factory-manufactured modules, reducing on-site constructionRolls-Royce SMR, GE-Hitachi and others — the UK's fastest-growing new build segment

03Major developers active in the UK market

  • EDF Energy — operates the UK's existing fleet and is delivering Hinkley Point C and Sizewell C (EPR/PWR technology).
  • Framatome — French nuclear engineering group supplying major components and fuel technology into EPR programmes.
  • GE Hitachi — BWR technology and SMR designs, active in UK SMR competition activity.
  • Rosatom — Russian state nuclear corporation, a major global developer (not currently active in UK new build given sanctions context).
  • Westinghouse — US-headquartered PWR technology and fuel supplier with a long UK supply chain presence.
  • Rolls-Royce SMR — UK-based SMR developer, one of the most active near-term UK supply chain opportunities for SMEs.

04What drives where a reactor gets built

  • Proximity to a large, reliable water source for cooling.
  • Grid connection capacity and proximity to demand centres.
  • Existing nuclear licensed site status (new build is heavily concentrated on or near existing nuclear sites).
  • Geological and seismic suitability.
  • Local community and existing workforce factors.

05Emerging technology worth watching

  • Generation IV designs — next-generation reactor concepts aiming for improved safety, efficiency and waste characteristics, still largely at research and early deployment stage.
  • Advanced SMRs — beyond first-generation SMRs, using novel coolants or fuel forms; earlier-stage supply chain opportunity than established PWR/BWR designs.
  • Fusion — a fundamentally different technology to fission, covered in NucCol's dedicated Introduction to Fusion Power guide.

06A realistic first step

Identify which developer and reactor type your target site actually usesDon't pitch generic "nuclear experience" — name the specific programme (Hinkley Point C, Sizewell C, a named SMR developer) and speak to what that technology's supply chain actually needs.
Match your capability to the technology, not just the sectorA component qualified for a PWR's primary circuit is not automatically relevant to an SMR's modular fabrication process — check before assuming transferability.
Track SMR developer supply chain events specificallySMR programmes are earlier-stage and actively mapping their future supply chains now — this is often a lower-competition entry point than established fleet suppliers.

07Frequently asked questions

Do I need to specialise in one reactor type?

Not necessarily — many components and services (fabrication, testing, civils, logistics) transfer across reactor types, but your pitch and qualification evidence should still be specific to the technology you're targeting.

Is SMR really different from a supply chain perspective?

Yes — SMRs are designed around factory-built modules rather than extensive on-site construction, which shifts demand towards precision manufacturing and modular fabrication capability rather than large-scale civils.
