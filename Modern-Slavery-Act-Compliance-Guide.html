Modern Slavery Act Compliance Guide

What your nuclear-sector customers expect from you, even if the Act doesn't technically apply to your business yet, explained in full.

6 min readLegal & Compliance

01What Section 54 requires

  • Applies to organisations with turnover of £36m or more
  • Requires an annual Modern Slavery Statement
  • Statement must be approved by the board and signed by a director
  • Statement must be published prominently on the company website
Most SME manufacturers sit below the £36m threshold — but your nuclear-sector customers almost certainly don't. Their obligations flow down through their supply chain, including to you, which is why this matters even if the Act itself doesn't technically require anything of your own business.

02What your customers will likely ask for

  • Confirmation that you don't use forced or child labour, directly or through your own suppliers
  • Basic visibility into your own labour supply chain — recruitment agencies, subcontractors, and how they're vetted
  • A statement or policy, even a simple one, showing you've considered the risk seriously rather than ticking a box

03A proportionate approach

You don't need a full statutory statement below the turnover threshold — but a short, honest policy showing you understand the risk and have basic checks in place will satisfy most customer due-diligence questionnaires, and can usually be written in an afternoon rather than requiring specialist input.

A simple approach that worksA one-page policy stating how you recruit staff, that you check agency workers' right-to-work status, and that you'd expect the same standards from your own suppliers, signed by a director, is usually more than sufficient for an SME below the reporting threshold.

04Frequently asked questions

We're well below £36m turnover — can we ignore this entirely?

Not in practice — your larger customers' own statements require them to check their supply chain, so expect to be asked, even if you have no statutory obligation yourself.

How often should we review our policy?

Annually is a sensible minimum, or sooner if your supply chain or recruitment practices change significantly.
