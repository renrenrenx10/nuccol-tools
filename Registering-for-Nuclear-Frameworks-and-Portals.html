A Practical Walkthrough

Registering for Nuclear Frameworks & Portals

AVLs, supplier portals and framework registration can look like a wall of paperwork from the outside. Here's what's actually involved, using the real portals and processes UK nuclear buyers use.

10 min readWinning Work

01What you're actually registering for

"Registering for a framework" usually means one of three things: joining a pre-qualification database that multiple buyers draw from (like Achilles UVDB), registering directly with one buyer's own supplier portal (many nuclear buyers use Atamis-powered portals), or registering on the government's central platform for public contracts (Find a Tender). Most established suppliers end up doing more than one, because different buyers use different systems.

An Approved Vendor List (AVL) is simply a buyer's own shortlist of suppliers who've been through their due diligence and can be invited to quote without repeating it every time. Getting onto one doesn't guarantee work — it means you're now eligible to be asked.

02Route 1 — Achilles UVDB (used widely across utilities and infrastructure)

Achilles UVDB is a pre-qualification database used by a wide range of buyers, including infrastructure and utilities clients. Registration is designed around a single questionnaire that collects your company information once, so buyers who use UVDB can pull your validated profile rather than each running their own separate checks.

Step 1 — Gather your company informationCompany name, address, website, tax number, registration number and a copy of your company registration certificate.
Step 2 — List what you supplyA clear description of your products and services, and which regions you can supply into.
Step 3 — Have your certificates and policies readyQuality management, environmental and health & safety certificates, plus corporate social responsibility, environmental management and QMS policies.
Step 4 — Provide financial and contact informationAnnual turnover for the last year, staff numbers, and named business and admin contacts.

Once submitted, your information is validated before your profile is published — Achilles offers online training webinars, a local Supplier Account Manager, and a product code guide to help you find the right classification for what you supply.

03Route 2 — a buyer's own supplier portal (e.g. Atamis-powered portals)

Many individual nuclear buyers run their own supplier portal rather than relying solely on a shared database like UVDB. A number of these run on the Atamis platform — each portal is independent to that one buying organisation, so registering with one buyer's portal doesn't carry over to another's, even if both happen to use the same underlying software.

StageWhat happens
Register a portal accountCreate an account against that specific buyer's portal (a unique link per organisation) — if you already work with other buyers on the same platform, you'll get a distinct username per portal rather than one login for all of them.
Complete your supplier profilePopulate company information the buyer requests, similar in shape to UVDB's checklist.
Find opportunities and express interestBrowse published opportunities; where a project is open for expressions of interest, a Register Interest button lets you flag interest and access supporting documentation packs.
Respond to selection questionnaires and tendersOnce invited (or once your expression of interest is accepted), you complete Selection Questionnaire responses and, later, full tender submissions through the same portal.
If a buyer has already invited you to tender directly, you don't need to register interest separately — the opportunity should already be visible under your account's proposals and quotes.

04Route 3 — Find a Tender (the government's central platform)

Find a Tender (the Central Digital Platform) is where UK public sector contract opportunities, including many nuclear ones, are advertised under the Procurement Act 2023. Registration is designed to be quick and reusable across multiple bids.

Step 1 — Register for a GOV.UK One LoginThis is your personal sign-in, used across government digital services.
Step 2 — Complete Central Digital Platform registrationRegister your organisation and receive your unique supplier identifier, which you'll quote on every future bid.
Step 3 — Keep your core business details currentYour registered details are reused across multiple bids, so keeping them accurate saves re-entering the same information every time.

05Social value — what buyers now expect alongside the paperwork

Since Procurement Policy Note 002/026 (the Social Value Model), central government buyers must, for most contracts, assess how a supplier will benefit communities — good jobs, skills and opportunities — alongside price and technical merit. It applies most strictly to central government contracts above £1m, but many public and quasi-public nuclear buyers now expect a social value response as standard regardless of the exact contract value.

  • Expect a specific award criterion scored alongside price and quality, not a tick-box afterthought.
  • Common themes: local employment, apprenticeships and skills, supply chain diversity, and community benefit in the areas where you operate.
  • Have a short, honest social value statement ready before you need one — retrofitting one under bid deadline pressure rarely reads well.
  • NucCol's own toolbox includes a Social Value Action Plan template and an Ethical Sourcing Policy template that map directly onto what buyers are looking for.

06Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Assuming one registration (say, UVDB) covers every buyer — most SMEs end up needing at least two of the three routes above.
✗Leaving certificates, policies and financial information to gather at the point of registering, rather than having a ready folder maintained year-round.
✗Registering once and letting the profile go stale — buyers use these portals to check you're still active and current, not just that you once qualified.
✗Treating social value as generic corporate wording rather than something specific and locally grounded — vague answers score poorly.

07Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be on Achilles UVDB to work in nuclear?

Not always — it depends which buyers you're targeting. Some nuclear buyers use UVDB, others run their own portal, and public sector opportunities may only require Find a Tender registration. Check what your target buyer actually uses before registering anywhere.

How long does registration usually take?

A well-prepared UVDB or portal registration, with your certificates and policies already gathered, can often be completed in a single sitting. The slow part is usually gathering the supporting documents, not filling in the form.

Does getting onto an AVL guarantee I'll be invited to quote?

No — it makes you eligible to be invited. Being visible, credible and easy to find (a good profile, current certificates, clear capability description) still matters for actually being approached.

Is social value only relevant for very large contracts?

The formal PPN 002/026 threshold is £1m for central government contracts, but many nuclear buyers apply similar expectations well below that threshold as good practice — worth having a social value answer ready regardless of contract size.
