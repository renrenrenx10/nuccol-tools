What SCRL and demand modelling actually mean for you

Demystifying Supply Chain Readiness

SCRL — Supply Chain Readiness Level — is NucCol's own methodology for understanding how ready UK manufacturers are for nuclear work, at scale. Here's what it actually measures, how it's built, and why it exists.

8 min readSupply Chain

01What SCRL actually is

SCRL stands for Supply Chain Readiness Level. It's a NucCol methodology, inspired by the idea of Technology Readiness Levels (TRLs) used in engineering and research, that gives a structured, RAG-rated (Red/Amber/Green) picture of how ready a company — or a whole sector — is to take on nuclear supply chain work.

The idea behind borrowing the TRL concept is simple: just as a technology moves through defined stages from early concept to proven, deployable product, a supplier's readiness for nuclear work can be tracked through defined stages too, rather than treated as a single yes/no judgement.

02What it assesses

  • Quality management maturity — how embedded and evidenced your quality system is, not just whether one exists on paper.
  • Operational capability and capacity — whether you have the physical and process capacity to deliver at the scale being asked of you.
  • Financial stability — whether your business is resilient enough to take on nuclear-scale contracts without undue risk.
  • Health & safety performance — your track record and systems for keeping people safe.
  • Workforce capacity and skills pipeline — whether you have, or can build, the people to deliver and sustain the work.
  • Sector-specific compliance — nuclear-specific standards and requirements relevant to your type of work.

03How it's scored, and where the data comes from

Each area is given a RAG rating, built from F4N assessment data, NucCol's supplier portal of 3,500+ companies, and targeted engagement with individual businesses where more detail is needed. The result is a version-controlled workbook and report — not a one-off snapshot, but something that can be tracked and updated as a company's readiness changes over time.

05How this relates to item-level quality grading

SCRL looks at a company's overall readiness as a business. That's a different, narrower question from how a specific part or component is graded for procurement purposes. That's where the NSC1/NSC2/NSC3/NN framework comes in — it grades the item, not the company:

GradeWhat it means
NSC1Highest nuclear safety class — most stringent procurement and verification controls
NSC2High nuclear safety relevance — significant additional controls apply
NSC3Some nuclear safety relevance — moderate additional controls
NN (Non-Nuclear)Standard commercial procurement controls apply, no nuclear-specific overlay

In practice, a company with a strong overall SCRL rating can still supply items across several different NSC grades — the two measures work together, not in place of each other.

06What demand modelling actually means

Demand modelling is the exercise of forecasting how much of a given capability the sector will need — broken down by commodity type, such as fabrication, machining, or electrical assembly — over the coming years, so that gaps can be identified and investment (in skills, capacity, certification) can be targeted before a shortage becomes a genuine bottleneck for the whole programme.

Why this matters to you as a supplierIf demand modelling shows a growing need in your commodity area, that's a strong, evidence-based signal for where to focus your own investment and capacity planning decisions over the next 12–24 months.

07Frequently asked questions

How do I find out my own company's SCRL rating?

SCRL draws directly on F4N assessment data and NucCol's supplier engagement — completing an F4N assessment is the fastest way to be counted and to see where you stand.

Is SCRL the same thing as F4N certification?

No. F4N certification is a recognised standard you achieve; SCRL is a broader readiness-tracking methodology that draws on F4N data alongside other sources.

What's the difference between SCRL and NSC grading again?

SCRL rates your company's overall readiness. NSC grading (NSC1/2/3/NN) rates the nuclear safety relevance of a specific item you supply. Both can apply to the same business at once.
