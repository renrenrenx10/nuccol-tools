National and Regional Support for UK Manufacturers

Funding & Grants Navigator

You don't need to fund everything yourself. From Innovate UK grants to your own regional Growth Hub, here's the real funding landscape available to UK manufacturing SMEs right now.

10 min readGrowth & Funding

01Why this is worth ten minutes of your time

Most SMEs default to self-funding growth, R&D and equipment because chasing grants feels like a distraction from the day job. In practice, a surprising amount of funding is designed specifically for manufacturers your size — and several sources can be combined rather than chosen between.

Since April 2024, receiving a grant no longer prevents you from also claiming R&D tax relief on the subsidised portion of the same project. Stacking sources — a grant, tax relief on the balance, and a skills programme for the team — is now standard practice, not a loophole.

03Nine things worth knowing before you apply

  • Check your local Growth Hub first. Many UKSPF grants are administered locally, so your regional Growth Hub is often the fastest way to see what's actually open right now.
  • Stack funding sources where you can. An Innovate UK grant, R&D tax credits on the balance, and a Skills Bootcamp for workforce development can often run alongside each other.
  • Grants and R&D tax relief are no longer mutually exclusive. Since April 2024 you can claim relief on the subsidised portion of a grant-funded project.
  • Made Smarter is time-limited. The national roll-out across English regions in 2025/26 has limited capacity — registering early at madesmarter.uk matters more than usual.
  • Innovate UK's open-access Smart Grants are currently paused. Themed competitions through the Innovation Funding Service (IFS) portal are the live route in for 2025/26.
  • KTPs have a strong success rate. They're less competitive than direct R&D grants and a good fit when you have a specific challenge that needs academic expertise.
  • UKSPF is in a transitional year. 2025/26 successor arrangements are expected via the Spending Review — worth a watching brief rather than assuming continuity.
  • The Growth & Skills Levy is changing. Expect new apprenticeship unit rules from April 2026, and a £2,000 incentive for under-25 apprenticeship starts from October 2026.
  • Most Catapult centres offer a free first conversation. A good, low-risk way to test whether a collaborative R&D route is worth pursuing before committing.

05A realistic path to actually getting funded

Step 1 — Start with your Growth Hub, not a grant searchYour regional Growth Hub acts as a single gateway to most local schemes, and can point you to what's genuinely open right now rather than what's simply listed online.
Step 2 — Separate 'advisory' from 'grant' supportSeveral organisations above (Growth Hubs, Innovate UK Business Growth, Business Wales) don't hand out money directly, but materially improve your odds with the schemes that do — don't skip them assuming only cash counts.
Step 3 — Match the funding type to what you actually needR&D and innovation work points toward Innovate UK, KTPs or a Catapult. Digital/automation adoption points toward Made Smarter. Workforce development points toward the Growth & Skills Levy and Skills Bootcamps.
Step 4 — Talk to your accountant about stackingSince grants and R&D tax relief can now be combined, involve whoever handles your tax position early, not after the grant funding decision is made.
Step 5 — Build a simple funding calendarTime-limited rounds (UKSPF, Made Smarter's regional roll-out, specific Growth Hub calls) reward planning ahead rather than reacting when you happen to see one advertised.

06Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Assuming grants and R&D tax relief are mutually exclusive — that restriction was lifted for subsidised spend from April 2024.
✗Searching nationally first and missing better-fit regional or local schemes administered through your own Growth Hub.
✗Treating advisory-only support (Growth Hubs, Business Wales, Innovate UK Business Growth) as not worth engaging with, when it often materially improves grant success rates.
✗Missing time-limited windows — Made Smarter's regional roll-out and UKSPF's transitional year both reward early registration over a wait-and-see approach.
✗Overlooking Knowledge Transfer Partnerships because they sound academic — they have a strong success rate precisely because they're less competitive than direct grants.

07Frequently asked questions

Can I combine a grant with R&amp;D tax relief on the same project?

Yes, since April 2024 — you can claim enhanced R&D tax relief on the portion of a project not covered by the grant, where previously the grant could block the claim entirely.

Are Innovate UK's Smart Grants still open?

The open-access Smart Grants have been paused for 2025/26. The live route in is through themed competitions on the Innovation Funding Service (IFS) portal, so check that regularly rather than only the general Smart Grants page.

What's the fastest way to find out what's available in my area?

Start with your regional Growth Hub or Combined Authority (see the regional matrix above) — they act as a single point of contact for most local and UKSPF-administered schemes, and can tell you what's currently open rather than what's simply listed.

Is UKSPF worth pursuing given it's described as transitional?

Yes — 2025/26 funding is still live even though successor arrangements for 2026/27 onwards are still being finalised through the Spending Review. Don't rule it out on the basis of the transition alone.

What if I don't have a specific R&amp;D project, just general growth ambitions?

Look first at Combined Authority and Growth Hub business support funds, Start Up Loans (if trading under 3 years), and Made Smarter if digital/automation adoption is part of the plan — these are typically more accessible than the R&D-focused schemes.
