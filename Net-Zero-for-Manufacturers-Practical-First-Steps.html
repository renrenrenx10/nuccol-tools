Practical First Steps

Net Zero for Manufacturers

Net zero doesn't have to start with a consultant and a five-figure invoice. Here's a realistic, SME-sized starting point &mdash; and what buyers are now actually asking for.

8 min readOperations & Skills

01Why this is landing on your desk now

98% of UK manufacturers are aware of the government's net zero target, and 77% believe achieving net zero by 2050 is realistic for their own business, according to Make UK's manufacturer research. Awareness isn't the hard part — knowing where to actually start is.

There's also a commercial driver, separate from any personal conviction on the issue: under Procurement Policy Note 06/21, government buyers must take account of a supplier's Carbon Reduction Plan on contracts above £5 million a year. Many nuclear buyers now ask for evidence of net zero thinking well below that threshold too, simply because it's become a standard part of due diligence.

02Start by measuring, not by promising

The single most useful first step is understanding your own emissions before committing to anything. Three realistic paths exist, in rough order of effort:

ApproachBest for
Free calculators (e.g. SME Climate Hub)Most SMEs starting out — a Small Business Carbon Calculator for a first estimate, an Advanced Business Carbon Calculator if you operate multiple sites, and a Scope 3 calculator once you're ready to look at your supply chain
Sector- or region-specific calculatorsBusinesses whose activities aren't well captured by general SME tools
A consultancy or specialist serviceWhen you need an auditable, third-party-verified figure — often once a specific customer or contract requires it
Use a consistent, recognised methodology — the GHG Protocol is the most widely used framework for greenhouse gas accounting, and most free calculators (including SME Climate Hub's) are built to align with it. Start simple with the data you already have, and refine year on year.

03Understanding Scope 1, 2 and 3 — in plain terms

Scope 1

Emissions you directly create — fuel burned in your own vehicles, gas used to heat your own building.

Scope 2

Emissions created generating the electricity you buy and use — even though you don't burn the fuel yourself.

Scope 3

Everything else in your value chain — emissions from what you buy, how it's delivered to you, and what happens to what you sell. Usually the largest and hardest category to measure, but increasingly what buyers ask about first.

04Quick wins that don't need capital investment

  • Energy management basics — knowing where and when you actually use energy is often more revealing (and cheaper to act on) than any equipment upgrade.
  • Heating, ventilation and refrigeration reviews — these systems are common, often-overlooked sources of avoidable waste in manufacturing environments.
  • Energy procurement — reviewing your tariff and exploring green tariffs can shift your reported footprint without any operational change at all.
  • Office equipment and lighting — smaller in scale than production-floor changes, but genuinely quick to act on.

05When it's time to think bigger

  • Energy storage, to smooth demand and make better use of on-site or contracted renewable generation.
  • Commissioning a dedicated energy efficiency project once quick wins are exhausted and the business case for capital investment is clear.
  • Setting a formal, science-aligned reduction target (the 1.5°C-aligned target-setting approach used by bodies like the SME Climate Hub) once you have a reliable baseline to measure progress against.

06What a Carbon Reduction Plan actually needs to contain

If a buyer asks for a Carbon Reduction Plan under PPN 06/21 (or a similar internal requirement), it follows a recognised technical standard, not a free-text essay:

  • Your current emissions footprint, split by Scope 1, 2 and (where relevant) 3.
  • The environmental management measures already in place.
  • A stated commitment to achieving Net Zero, and by when.
  • Specific carbon reduction targets and the measures you'll take to reach them.
A published template and technical standard for the Carbon Reduction Plan already exist under PPN 06/21 — use the official structure rather than writing one from scratch, since buyers are checking against that exact standard.

07Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Waiting for a customer to demand a Carbon Reduction Plan before starting to measure anything, leaving no runway to build a credible baseline.
✗Overcomplicating the first measurement — a simple SME calculator, done consistently, beats an ambitious methodology abandoned after one year.
✗Focusing only on Scope 1 and 2 (your own operations) when buyers increasingly ask about Scope 3 (your supply chain) first.
✗Treating net zero purely as a compliance exercise, and missing the quick-win cost savings that usually come alongside emissions reductions.

08Frequently asked questions

Do I legally have to report my emissions?

Only certain larger companies are required to under Streamlined Energy and Carbon Reporting (SECR) rules. Most SMEs aren't legally required to, but a growing number of buyers now ask for it as a condition of doing business, regardless of the legal threshold.

What's the difference between PPN 06/21 and the Social Value Model (PPN 002/026)?

They're related but distinct: PPN 06/21 is specifically about a supplier's Carbon Reduction Plan and Net Zero commitment. The Social Value Model covers broader community and social outcomes, of which environmental sustainability is only one theme.

Is a free calculator good enough, or do I need a consultant?

A free, GHG Protocol-aligned calculator is a genuinely credible starting point for most SMEs and buyers. A consultant becomes worthwhile once you need third-party verification, or your operations are too complex for a general tool to capture accurately.

Where do I start if I have absolutely no idea what our footprint is?

Start with a free Small Business Carbon Calculator (SME Climate Hub is a commonly used one) using whatever energy and fuel data you already have from bills and invoices. An imperfect first measurement is more useful than no measurement at all.

Building this into your F4N readiness?

Environmental and sustainability evidence increasingly feeds into buyer due diligence alongside your F4N assessment — worth having your Carbon Reduction Plan ready before it's asked for.
