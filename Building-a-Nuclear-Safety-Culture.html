Building a Nuclear Safety Culture

The INPO/WANO 'Ten Traits' framework, explained for an SME context.

6 min readSafety Culture

01Individual Commitment to Safety

Personal accountabilityEveryone knows their own role in keeping things safe — not just 'someone else's job'.
Questioning attitudePeople are encouraged to ask 'is this right?' rather than assume it is.
Effective safety communicationConcerns and lessons are shared openly, not filtered upward.

02Management Commitment to Safety

Leadership safety values and actionsLeaders visibly prioritise safety, especially when it's inconvenient.
Decision-makingSafety is weighed explicitly in decisions, not assumed to be 'fine'.
Respectful work environmentPeople feel able to raise issues without fear of blame.

03Management Systems

Continuous learningNear-misses and mistakes are treated as learning opportunities.
Problem identification and resolutionIssues are tracked to genuine closure, not just logged.
Environment for raising concernsThere's a clear, safe route to raise a concern at any level.
Work processesProcedures reflect how work is actually done, and are kept current.

Safety culture is assessed, not assumed
