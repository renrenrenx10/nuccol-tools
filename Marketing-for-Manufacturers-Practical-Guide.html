Practical Guide for SMEs Without a Marketing Team

Marketing for Manufacturers

You don't need a marketing department to win nuclear work. You need a clear message, a few consistent channels, and proof that you can deliver.

7 min readGrowth & Marketing

01Start with your value proposition

Before any channel or campaign, be able to answer one question in a single sentence: why should a nuclear buyer choose you over the next supplier on the list? That might be a certification, a niche capability, your location, your track record, or your capacity to scale.

02Four channels that punch above their weight

ChannelWhat it's forEffort
WebsiteYour credibility anchor — buyers will check it before calling youSet up once, refresh quarterly
LinkedInVisibility with procurement and engineering contacts, sharing wins15–30 min/week
Events (Meet the Buyer, Summits)Face-to-face relationship building — still the highest-converting channelA few days/quarter
Email nurtureStaying front-of-mind between opportunitiesOne update/month

03Proof beats polish

Nuclear buyers are risk-averse by design. A short, specific case study — what you did, for whom (even anonymised), and the outcome — will do more work than an expensive brochure. If you have an F4N certificate, a quality accreditation, or a notable customer, put it above the fold on your homepage.

04A realistic quarterly rhythm

WhenActivity
Month 1Refresh website proof points; publish one LinkedIn case study
Month 2Attend or follow up from one Meet the Buyer style event
Month 3Send one email update to your contact list; review what worked

05Measuring what matters

  • Enquiries that mention a specific capability (means your message is landing)
  • Repeat engagement from the same buyer contact over time
  • Website visits from procurement-relevant searches, not just traffic volume
