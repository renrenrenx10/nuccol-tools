Getting the Most from 'Meet the Buyer' Events

A little preparation turns a Meet the Buyer event from a networking day into a genuine pipeline of opportunities. Here's how to prepare, work the room, and follow up properly.

8 min readGrowth & Events

01Why these events matter more in this sector

Nuclear procurement relationships tend to be built over months, not minutes — buyers are looking for suppliers they can trust with safety-relevant work, and that trust is built through repeated, credible contact more than through a single strong pitch. Meet the Buyer events are one of the few places you get that contact face to face, which is exactly why the preparation below matters so much.

02Before the day

  • Research which buyers are attending and shortlist the 5–6 conversations that matter most to your business, rather than trying to speak to everyone.
  • Prepare a one-minute version of your value proposition, and practise saying it out loud — it should sound natural, not rehearsed.
  • Bring evidence: certifications, case studies, capacity data — not just a brochure. Buyers remember specifics.
  • Book meeting slots in advance where the event allows it, rather than relying entirely on the day itself.
  • Prepare two or three genuine questions about the buyer's upcoming needs — this signals real interest, not just a sales pitch.

03On the day

  • Lead with what you do and for whom, not a company history — buyers are time-pressured and want the headline first.
  • Ask buyers what their upcoming challenges are, and listen more than you pitch — the best conversations are two-way.
  • Take specific notes on every conversation: name, need, and agreed next step. Memory alone will not survive a full day of meetings.
  • Don't try to close on the day — the realistic goal is a warm, specific follow-up, not a signed contract.

04After the event

  • Follow up within 48 hours while the conversation is still fresh for the buyer, not just for you.
  • Reference something specific they said — it signals you were genuinely listening, not sending a template.
  • Log every contact somewhere durable, even a simple spreadsheet, so nothing falls through the cracks over the following weeks.
  • Set a calendar reminder to follow up again in 4–6 weeks if you haven't heard back — persistence here is expected, not pushy.

05Common mistakes SMEs make here

✗Trying to speak to every buyer at the event instead of preparing properly for the ones that matter most.
✗Leading with company history instead of the buyer's problem.
✗Letting notes live only in memory, then losing the detail by the time follow-up happens.
✗Giving up after one unanswered follow-up email.

06Frequently asked questions

I've never attended one of these events before — is it worth going without an appointment?

Yes. Many events allow drop-in conversations, and simply being visible and prepared is valuable even without pre-booked slots.

How soon after an event should I expect to hear back?

Realistically, weeks rather than days — treat the event as the start of a relationship, not a single transaction.
