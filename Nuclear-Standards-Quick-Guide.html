Quick Guide

Nuclear Supply Chain Quality Standards

What ISO 9001 and ISO 19443 actually require, and how to know which grade of quality applies to your work.

6 min readStandards & Compliance

01ISO 9001 vs ISO 19443 — what's the difference?

ISO 9001 is the standard quality management foundation most manufacturers already know. ISO 19443 builds directly on top of it — it doesn't replace ISO 9001, it adds nuclear-specific requirements for organisations supplying items or services that are Important To Nuclear Safety (ITNS).

AreaISO 9001ISO 19443
FoundationGeneric quality management systemRequires ISO 9001 as a base, then adds to it
FocusCustomer satisfaction, continual improvementNuclear safety culture, independence, traceability
GradingOne standard for all productsFour graded levels of rigour (see below)
OversightInternal/external auditAdds nuclear-specific surveillance and reporting duties

02What does ITNS mean?

ITNS stands for Important To Nuclear Safety. It's the label applied to any item, component or service whose failure could affect nuclear safety. If what you supply is classed as ITNS, ISO 19443's extra requirements apply to you — the grading below tells you how strictly.

GradeWhat it means
Grade 1Highest rigour — directly safety-critical items
Grade 2High rigour — significant safety relevance
Grade 3Moderate rigour — some safety relevance
Grade 4 / Non-ITNSStandard ISO 9001 requirements apply

03Five things ISO 19443 asks beyond ISO 9001

  • A documented nuclear safety culture, not just a quality policy statement.
  • Independence and objectivity for anyone carrying out verification or inspection.
  • Full traceability of parts, materials and records — who touched what, and when.
  • A formal process for reporting and escalating safety-significant issues up the supply chain.
  • Evidence that sub-tier suppliers are also flowing down the same expectations.

05Getting started

Stage 1 — Know your baselineConfirm you hold (or are working towards) ISO 9001. This is the non-negotiable foundation.
Stage 2 — Understand your ITNS statusAsk your customer, or your F4N assessor, whether your work is likely to be classed as ITNS and at what grade.
Stage 3 — Gap-assess against ISO 19443Compare your current QMS against the five areas above before committing to formal certification.
Stage 4 — Build traceability into daily practiceTraceability is usually the biggest practical gap — start recording it now, even before certification.
