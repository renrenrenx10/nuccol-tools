Quick wins for small teams

Lean Manufacturing

You don't need a lean transformation programme to get real results. Five small, practical changes you can start this week, with the detail behind each one.

7 min readOperations

01Run a 5S pass on one area

5S stands for Sort, Straighten, Shine, Standardise, Sustain. Pick one workbench or cell — not the whole factory — and work through each step: remove what isn't needed, arrange what remains logically, clean it properly, agree a standard for how it should look, and build a habit of maintaining it. Start this week, on one small area, and let success there build the case for doing more.

02Put up a simple visual KPI board

One whiteboard, three or four numbers that actually matter — output, quality, on-time delivery, or whatever drives your business — updated daily or weekly where it can be seen by the whole team, not just management. The value isn't sophistication, it's visibility: problems get noticed and acted on faster when everyone can see the trend, not just read about it in a meeting.

03Write SOPs for your top three recurring problems

Not everything needs a written procedure — just the three issues that cost you the most time or cause the most rework. A one-page SOP that a new starter could follow without asking for help is worth more than a comprehensive manual nobody reads.

04Use a simple root-cause method

Ask 'why' five times on your next defect or delay before acting — most first answers describe a symptom, not the actual cause. This costs nothing but a few minutes of discipline, and it prevents the same problem recurring next month in a slightly different form.

A worked exampleDefect found → why? Wrong tolerance set → why? Operator used the wrong job sheet → why? Job sheets aren't clearly labelled by revision → why? No standard for revision control on the shop floor. The fix here is a labelling standard, not just retraining one operator.

05Hold a 10-minute daily huddle

Same time, same three questions: what went well, what didn't, what needs help. Kept strictly to ten minutes and standing up if possible, this catches small problems while they're still small, rather than letting them accumulate into a bigger issue by the end of the week.

06Why this matters for F4N and CSIP

These are exactly the kind of practical, evidenced improvements an F4N assessment and a Continuous Improvement Plan (CSIP) look for — small, real changes that compound over time, not a big-bang transformation programme that's hard to sustain.

07Frequently asked questions

Do I need lean consultants to do any of this?

No — everything above can be started this week with no external help and no budget. External support can add value later, but it isn't a prerequisite to begin.

How do I know if it's working?

Your KPI board is the answer — if the numbers you chose to track are moving in the right direction over a few weeks, it's working.
