A CFSI awareness guide

Spotting Counterfeit Parts

Counterfeit, Fraudulent and Suspect Items (CFSI) are a real and growing risk in complex supply chains — here's what to watch for, and exactly what to do if you're suspicious.

7 min readSupply Chain

01What CFSI actually covers

CategoryMeaning
CounterfeitDeliberately made to look like a genuine, approved item, without authorisation
FraudulentMisrepresented origin, certification or test results, whether or not the physical item itself is genuine
SuspectNot confirmed genuine — insufficient evidence either way, and treated with caution until resolved

02Why this risk exists at all

Complex, multi-tier supply chains create opportunities for counterfeit or misrepresented items to enter the chain, sometimes unknowingly even to the immediate seller. This isn't about assuming bad faith everywhere — it's about building routine checks that catch the rare cases before they cause harm.

03Warning signs at purchasing stage

  • Price significantly below market rate for the specification, with no clear explanation
  • Vague or unverifiable supply chain history for the item
  • Pressure to bypass normal procurement or verification steps to secure a deal quickly
  • Documentation that looks inconsistent, incomplete, or recently altered

04Warning signs at goods-receipt

  • Packaging, labelling or markings that don't quite match the expected standard or previous deliveries
  • Physical inconsistencies — finish, weight, dimensions — versus known-good parts you've received before
  • Missing or incomplete certificates of conformity accompanying the delivery
Warning signs are not proof. They're reasons to stop and check — not to make accusations. Most flagged items turn out to have an innocent explanation, and the process exists precisely to resolve that quickly and fairly.

05What to do if you're suspicious

StepAction
1. Stop & quarantineIsolate the item physically — don't use it, and don't return it to the supplier yet.
2. ReportNotify your quality function and your customer promptly, following your own reporting procedure.
3. Don't investigate aloneCFSI investigations follow specific protocols involving specialist input — this isn't a task to handle informally.
4. Follow throughTrack the item and the outcome to closure, and feed the learning back into your purchasing and goods-receipt process.

06Frequently asked questions

What if I'm wrong and the item turns out to be genuine?

That's a normal and expected outcome of a healthy CFSI process — raising a concern that turns out unfounded is far preferable to missing a genuine one.

Do I need special training to spot CFSI?

Basic awareness, like the warning signs above, is enough for most staff. Specialist investigation, if a concern is confirmed, is a separate step handled by trained personnel.
