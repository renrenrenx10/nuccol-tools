About DangerMouse
How the model works, where the data comes from, and how to interpret results
v2.0 · Updated June 2026 · NuCCoL
☢ What is DangerMouse?

DangerMouse is NuCCoL's nuclear supply chain demand model. It takes a construction programme — one or more reactor developers, each with a start date, number of concurrent builds, and number of consecutive builds — and calculates how much of each commodity the UK supply chain will need to deliver, and when.

The output is a quarterly demand profile across the full build timeline, broken down by commodity family (e.g. Pumps, Valves, Steel & Civil) and category (e.g. Reactor Coolant Pumps, Motor-Operated Valves). This helps NuCCoL identify where supply chain gaps are likely to emerge and when intervention may be needed.

📂 Where does the data come from?

The commodity demand data was compiled by NuCCoL from a wide range of nuclear construction programmes and reactor vendor documentation. Two reference plant datasets underpin the model:

GW Plant Baseline

Represents a large-scale gigawatt-class pressurised water reactor. Demand figures are drawn from GW-scale construction programme data, reconciled and validated across multiple sources. Build duration: 10 years / 40 quarters.

SMR Plant Baseline

Represents a small modular reactor of approximately 300–470 MWe. Where real SMR-specific data existed it was used directly. Where gaps remained, GW figures were scaled down using a validated scaling methodology. Build duration: 7 years / 28 quarters.

Both datasets were compiled entirely by NuCCoL. All sources have been reviewed, normalised, and quality-checked. Data is stored in SharePoint and loaded into DangerMouse at runtime.

📊 How do I read the results?

Demand results show the amount of each commodity category required per quarter per calendar year. The chart view (accessed via View Chart) shows this as a stacked bar chart grouped by commodity family — 10 bars at the top level. Clicking a family bar drills into its constituent categories.

Units vary by category — pumps are counted in units (No.), concrete in cubic metres (m³), steel in tonnes (t), cable in metres (m). The unit for each category is shown in the Products screen. Do not add totals across categories with different units — the KPI total shown is a raw sum for indicative scale only.

⚠ IMPORTANT — Model limitations

DangerMouse is a planning and horizon-scanning tool, not a procurement specification. Demand figures represent NuCCoL's best estimate based on available data and are subject to change as programme information matures. Figures should be treated as indicative order-of-magnitude estimates only. Always validate against the latest developer programme data before using for commercial decisions.
