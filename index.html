About DangerMouse
How the model works, where the data comes from, and how to interpret results
v2.0 · Updated June 2026 · NuCCoL
☢ What is DangerMouse?

DangerMouse is NuCCoL's nuclear supply chain demand model. It takes a construction programme — one or more reactor developers, each with a start date, number of concurrent builds, and number of consecutive builds — and calculates how much of each commodity the UK supply chain will need to deliver, and when.

The output is a quarterly demand profile across the full build timeline, broken down by commodity family (e.g. Pumps, Valves, Steel & Civil) and category (e.g. Reactor Coolant Pumps, Motor-Operated Valves). This helps NuCCoL identify where supply chain gaps are likely to emerge and when intervention may be needed.

📂 Where does the data come from?

DangerMouse v3 is built from Reactor_BOM_DSE_Mapping_v3.xlsx — 18 named reactor designs, each mapped component-by-component to its official design-basis document (DCD/FSAR/PCSR), with page-level citations. On top of the 18 named designs sit two generic composites — Generic_GW_v2 and Generic_SMR_v2 — each the average of its class, used when you want a representative plant rather than a specific vendor design.

GW-shaped plants (9 designs)

ABWR, AP1000, APR1400, ESBWR, HPR1000, UK_EPR, US_APWR, US_EPR + Generic_GW_v2. Build duration: 10 years / 40 quarters.

SMR-shaped plants (11 designs)

AP300, AP600, BWRX300, Candu, Holtec SMR-300, NuScale US460 & VOYGR, RR_SMR, Terrapower Natrium, X Energy + Generic_SMR_v2. Build duration: 7 years / 28 quarters.

Every plant runs on real component-level quantities where they exist. Where a category had no trustworthy quantity anywhere in that reactor's own data, it was filled from the closest analogue reactor (same class first, scaled by MWe for bulk-unit categories) — see the Confidence column on the Products screen for which is which, per category.

🗂 How is the data organised?

The source data's own taxonomy (Commodity Group → Plant Capability → Component) was rolled up onto a Family → Category scheme, stopping one level above individual named components — "Valves → Check Valves", not "Valves → Check Valves → Reactor Fuel Check Valve 01" — because that's the grain demand modelling actually needs.

🔍 How trustworthy is each number?

Every category on the Products screen carries a confidence badge, computed from an audit across all 20 plants:

● Mostly native— summed from that reactor's own trustworthy rows: official-document extraction, or old-model data with a recovered HIGH/MEDIUM confidence tier.
◐ Partially native— some plants have native data for this category, others needed a cross-reactor analogue.
○ Mostly analogue/generic— borrowed from the nearest matching reactor (or averaged for the generic composites), scaled by MWe where the unit is bulk (m³, t, m). Treat as directional, not precise.

The full row-level audit — every quantity's source, the fuzzy-match confidence tier recovered from the old NuCCoL model, and every cross-reactor donor/scale-factor decision — is documented alongside this build in DangerMouse_Rebuild_Review.

📊 How do I read the results?

Demand results show the amount of each category required per quarter per calendar year. The chart view (accessed via View Chart) shows this as a stacked bar chart grouped by family — 21 bars at the top level. Clicking a family bar drills into its constituent categories, and each category's info panel now also shows its data-provenance confidence, not just capacity and lead time.

Units vary by category — pumps are counted in units (No.), concrete in cubic metres (m³), steel in tonnes (t), cable in metres (m). Do not add totals across categories with different units — the KPI total shown is a raw sum for indicative scale only.

⚠ IMPORTANT — Model limitations

DangerMouse is a planning and horizon-scanning tool, not a procurement specification. Demand figures represent NuCCoL's best estimate based on available data and are subject to change as programme information matures. Quarter-phasing is a shared shape per plant class (GW/SMR), not a real developer-specific build programme — there's no way to know how any individual developer will actually phase their build. Categories flagged "Mostly analogue/generic" are directional, not precise. Always validate against the latest programme data before using for commercial decisions.
